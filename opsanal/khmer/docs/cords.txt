The CORD field presented a special problem. This was a latitude/longitude field, which was
coded into a 64-bit double word, also known as 8-bytes or two full binary words. The layout
of each of this field is as shown below.
aabbbbbb bbcccccc cccccccc cccccccc deffffff ffgggggg gggggggg gggggggg
aa - Two zero bits, unused.
bbbbbbbb - An 8-bit unsigned binary number indicating the degrees of
latitude measured from the south pole. If the binary number is greater than
ninety, then ninety is subtracted from the binary number and the remainder is
the latitude in the Northern Hemisphere. If the binary number is less than
ninety, then the binary number is subtracted from ninety and the remainder is
the latitude in the Southern Hemisphere. If the binary number is equal to
ninety, then the latitude is zero (the equator).
cccccccccccccccccccccc - This is a 22-bit binary number, coded in
powers-of-a-half notation, that represents the minutes and seconds of the
latitude as a fraction of a degree.
d - This is the longitude hemisphere flag.
e - One zero bit.
ffffffff - The longitude measured in degrees from 0 degrees (Greenwich).
This is represented as an 8-bit unsigned binary number.
9999999999999999999999 - This is a 22-bit binary number, coded in
powers-of-a-half notation, that represents the minutes and seconds of the
longitude as a fraction of a degree.
Latitude/Longitude on the translated files is shown as ddmmssxDDDMMSSX in which:
dd is the 2-digit degrees of Latitude
rnm is the minutes of Latitude
ss is the seconds of Latitude

X equals either East or West.
The example of the latitude of 49501 ON would mean that 49 is the degrees of latitude, 50 is 

the minutes, 10 is the seconds, and N is North. 

Similarly, the longitude of 1032504E would mean 103 degrees, 25 minutes, 4 seconds, 

East.
